## O-P001

Final Harmonized 4W Scoring Rubric

## O-P002

AI implementation guide for ED CT/CTA abdomen-pelvis clinical indications

## O-P003

Purpose: assign four independent binary variables—WHAT, WHEN, WHERE, and WHY—to each clinical indication. Each element is scored 1 when satisfied and 0 when not satisfied. Total score = WHAT + WHEN + WHERE + WHY (0–4). The primary QI outcome defines a complete indication as a total score of 3 or 4.

## O-P004

1. Core AI scoring principles

## O-P005

Score only information documented in the indication. Do not invent symptoms, timing, anatomy, or diagnostic intent.

## O-P006

Use clinically meaningful interpretation rather than rigid keyword matching, but inference must be narrow and reproducible.

## O-P007

Score each W independently, subject to the hierarchy and exceptions below.

## O-P008

A suspected diagnosis usually supplies WHY and may supply WHERE when it clearly localizes an organ/system; it does not automatically supply WHAT.

## O-P009

An established active diagnosis may supply WHAT and WHERE. It supplies WHY only when the indication communicates reassessment, progression, extent, complication, treatment response, source, or another diagnostic purpose.

## O-P010

Historical conditions do not rescue missing information about the current problem unless explicitly linked to the active diagnostic target.

## O-P011

When wording remains genuinely ambiguous after applying these rules, flag the case for human adjudication rather than silently inferring.

## O-P012

2. WHAT — Current clinical problem

## O-P013

WHAT = a documented current symptom, sign, clinically meaningful objective finding, active syndrome/problem, traumatic event, device malfunction, or established active diagnosis.

## O-P014

Score WHAT = 1 when

## O-P015

A current symptom/sign is documented: pain, fever, vomiting, hematuria, rectal bleeding, distention, tenderness, drainage, swelling, etc.

## O-P016

A meaningful current objective finding is documented: elevated lactate, leukocytosis, tachycardia, hypotension, ascites, abnormal imaging finding being actively evaluated, etc.

## O-P017

An established active diagnosis/problem is stated: pancreatitis, SBO, intussusception, UTI, sepsis, peritonitis, diverticulitis, etc.

## O-P018

A device malfunction/problem is present: dislodged, leaking, obstructed, not draining, not flushing.

## O-P019

A traumatic event/mechanism is stated: fall, MVA, assault.

## O-P020

A known abnormality is explicitly being reassessed for persistence/worsening/progression.

## O-P021

Score WHAT = 0 when

## O-P022

The only clinical entity is suspected: “concern for,” “rule out,” “possible,” “?”, “evaluate for.”

## O-P023

The indication contains only historical disease without a current active problem.

## O-P024

The wording is only a generic target such as lesion, abnormality, process, or area without a meaningful active finding.

## O-P025

Important: do not count the same suspected diagnosis as both WHAT and WHY merely because it is clinically plausible.

## O-P026

3. WHEN — Timing or acuity

## O-P027

WHEN = explicit onset, duration, acuity, or a clearly linked temporal relationship for the active problem.

## O-P028

Score WHEN = 1 when

## O-P029

Explicit duration/onset: “3 days,” “since yesterday,” “this morning,” “for 2 weeks,” “earlier today.”

## O-P030

Explicit acuity of the active problem: “acute abdominal pain,” “acute-on-chronic pain,” “sudden onset.”

## O-P031

A transition occurs during the current encounter: “became unresponsive in the waiting room.”

## O-P032

A specifically dated/clearly recent procedure is directly linked to an evolving postoperative problem or suspected complication.

## O-P033

A recent intervention is explicitly linked to reassessment of persistence/worsening: e.g., abscess drained 1–2 weeks ago, now evaluating persistent/worsening abscess.

## O-P034

“Since discharge” or another explicit temporal anchor links the active problem to a defined event.

## O-P035

Usually WHEN = 0

## O-P036

“recent,” “worsening,” “increased,” “intermittent,” “persistent,” “ongoing,” “continued,” “recurrent,” “episode,” or “downtrending” alone.

## O-P037

Timing of a historical procedure/diagnosis that is not linked to onset or evolution of the current problem.

## O-P038

“postprandial” (trigger/pattern, not duration/acuity).

## O-P039

The word “acute” embedded in a standardized diagnosis such as AKI/ARF; require separate timing/acuity information.

## O-P040

4. WHERE — Clinically useful localization

## O-P041

WHERE = clinically useful anatomic localization of the current problem or diagnostic target. This is the most hierarchy-dependent W.

## O-P042

A. Localizable symptoms

## O-P043

A localizable symptom must itself be meaningfully localized: RLQ, LLQ, RUQ, epigastric, suprapubic, right flank, left-sided, diffuse/generalized, etc.

## O-P044

“Abdominal pain” alone → WHERE = 0.

## O-P045

“Back pain” alone → WHERE = 0.

## O-P046

Abdominal tenderness without a quadrant/region or explicit diffuse/generalized distribution → WHERE = 0.

## O-P047

If an unlocalized localizable symptom such as abdominal pain is present, systemic/nonlocalizable findings generally do not rescue WHERE.

## O-P048

B. Non-localizable symptoms/findings

## O-P049

When there is no competing unlocalized localizable symptom, intrinsically non-localizable presentations may satisfy WHERE by default because no more specific anatomic localization is reasonably expected.

## O-P050

Examples: sepsis, fever, hypotension, post-arrest state, lactic acidosis, anemia/Hgb drop, altered mental status/unresponsiveness, generalized weakness, ascites, hyperkalemia, nausea/vomiting.

## O-P051

Example: “rule out obstruction, nausea/vomiting with poor PO intake for 2 days” → WHERE = 1. The GI symptom context supports bowel/GI obstruction, and nausea/vomiting are non-localizable.

## O-P052

C. Specific diagnoses / organ-system localization

## O-P053

A sufficiently specific diagnosis, syndrome, or clinical entity may satisfy WHERE when it unambiguously identifies a clinically meaningful organ system or anatomic compartment.

## O-P079

D. Strict symptom hierarchy and historical context

## O-P080

Historical disease alone does not rescue an unlocalized current localizable symptom: “abdominal pain, history of pancreatitis” → WHERE = 0.

## O-P081

Broad “abdominal/intra-abdominal infection” without a quadrant/region, specific organ, or specific surgical site → WHERE = 0.

## O-P082

“Concern for abdominal infection post surgery” with no type/site of surgery → WHERE = 0.

## O-P083

The previously considered rule allowing a linked procedure/device/pathology to automatically override unlocalized pain is NOT part of the final rubric.

## O-P084

E. Narrow coherent-pattern exceptions

## O-P085

A clinically coherent symptom pattern plus an explicitly suspected pathology may provide localization when the relationship is strong and specific.

## O-P086

Example: “abdominal pain radiating to back, concern for nephrolithiasis” → WHERE = 1 because the radiation pattern plus nephrolithiasis/renal-colic pathway provides coherent GU localization.

## O-P087

Do not generalize this exception to weak associations; flag uncertain cases for adjudication.

## O-P088

F. Objective imaging localization

## O-P089

A documented objective imaging finding may satisfy WHERE when it anatomically localizes the active process.

## O-P090

Example: “possible developing obstruction, scattered air-fluid levels on KUB” → WHERE = 1 because the KUB finding localizes the active process to bowel/GI tract.

## O-P091

G. Procedural/device contextual localization

## O-P092

When there is no competing unlocalized localizable symptom, a specific anatomic/procedural/device context may localize a suspected complication if the wording reasonably links the target to that site.

## O-P093

“Extensive spine surgical history, evaluate for abscess” → WHERE = 1.

## O-P094

“History of cholecystectomy, biliary drain in place, evaluate for abscess” → WHERE = 1.

## O-P095

Generic or unrelated history does not establish WHERE: “history of colon cancer, evaluate for abscess” → WHERE = 0 unless the abscess is otherwise localized.

## O-P096

H. Other WHERE exceptions

## O-P097

Localization-by-imaging: WHERE may be 1 when the process is inherently unknown in location and imaging is specifically used to localize it, e.g., foreign-body ingestion.

## O-P098

Device type/site can localize an active device problem: nephrostomy tube, biliary drain, etc.

## O-P099

Bare trauma mechanism does not satisfy WHERE unless a body region is supplied.

## O-P100

Rectal bleeding/BRBPR/hematochezia may provide clinically useful lower-GI/rectal-output localization unless a competing unlocalized localizable symptom triggers the hierarchy.

## O-P101

Positive Murphy sign or McBurney point satisfies WHERE.

## O-P102

5. WHY — Diagnostic purpose

## O-P103

WHY = the reason imaging is being obtained: a specific diagnostic question, suspected diagnosis/complication, source/cause, extent/progression, treatment response, or reassessment.

## O-P104

Score WHY = 1 when

## O-P105

A specific suspected diagnosis/differential is stated: appendicitis, SBO, abscess, stone, pancreatitis, cholangitis, bile leak, etc.

## O-P106

A source/cause question is explicit: source of sepsis, unknown source, cause of elevated lactate.

## O-P107

A specific device question is stated: position, obstruction, leakage, malfunction, infection.

## O-P108

A known abnormality is explicitly being reassessed for progression, persistence, resolution, extent, or treatment response.

## O-P109

A specific complication is being evaluated and is clearly linked to an active disease/procedure/treatment.

## O-P110

A diagnostically specific syndrome/sign/pathway communicates purpose: renal colic, positive Murphy sign, complicated UTI/diverticulitis, McBurney point.

## O-P111

Trauma mechanism (fall/MVA/assault) inherently communicates evaluation for traumatic injury.

## O-P112

A coherent active complication sequence clearly communicates the diagnostic purpose even without the words “concern for,” but use this narrowly.

## O-P113

WHY = 0 when

## O-P114

The indication merely lists symptoms/findings without a diagnostic question.

## O-P115

The wording is generic: “evaluate further,” “rule out any abnormality,” “rule out acute process,” “evaluate surgical site,” “evaluate nephrostomy tubes,” or unanchored “concern for complications.”

## O-P116

The proposed WHY would require inferring a diagnosis solely from a recognizable symptom pattern. Example: coffee-ground emesis + dark stools alone does not automatically create WHY.

## O-P117

An established diagnosis is merely restated without indicating what imaging is meant to assess.

## O-P256

8. Recommended AI decision sequence

## O-P257

1. Identify all current symptoms, signs, objective findings, active diagnoses, trauma events, and device problems → score WHAT.

## O-P258

2. Identify explicit onset/duration/acuity or a clearly linked temporal event → score WHEN.

## O-P259

3. For WHERE, first ask whether a localizable current symptom is present. If yes, require meaningful localization unless a narrow documented exception applies.

## O-P260

4. If no competing unlocalized localizable symptom exists, assess non-localizable presentations, specific organ-system diagnoses, device/procedural context, and objective imaging localization.

## O-P261

5. Identify the explicit diagnostic question, suspected diagnosis, source/cause, complication, reassessment, progression, or device question → score WHY.

## O-P262

6. Calculate total = WHAT + WHEN + WHERE + WHY.

## O-P263

7. If two rules conflict or the linkage requires more than narrow clinical inference, output “ADJUDICATION REQUIRED” with the disputed W(s).

## O-P264

9. Suggested structured AI output

## O-P265

For each indication, return: WHAT (0/1) + brief evidence; WHEN (0/1) + brief evidence; WHERE (0/1) + brief evidence; WHY (0/1) + brief evidence; TOTAL (0–4); ADJUDICATION (Yes/No). The evidence should quote or closely paraphrase only the minimum phrase that drove each decision.

## O-P266

10. Guardrails for model training

## O-P267

Do not use patient age/sex to infer missing Ws.

## O-P268

Do not infer a diagnosis merely because symptoms are suggestive, except for explicitly defined narrow coherent-pattern exceptions.

## O-P269

Do not let historical disease automatically supply WHAT, WHEN, WHERE, or WHY.

## O-P270

Do not treat every occurrence of “acute,” “recent,” “worsening,” or “post-op” as WHEN.

## O-P271

Do not treat every abdominal/GI-related word as WHERE; follow the hierarchy.

## O-P272

Do not make a generic device evaluation WHY-positive without a specific device question.

## O-P273

Prefer human adjudication over expanding an exception ad hoc.
